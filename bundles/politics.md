j ITALIAN IMMIGRANTS. MANY NOW ON ELLIS ISLAND WILL BE DEPORTED. New York, March 27. At least four hundred of the 2,000 Italian immigrants who were landed on Ellis Island on Sunday and Monday will be deported, as they have not the means to subsist upon for thirty days. The law requires each immigrant to have at least $30 or to have some relative In America before he is admitted. Commissioner Senner attributes the large migration of Italians to the Italian invasion of Abyssinia, to which most Italian peasants are averse. They do not wish to be drafted into the army and so to avoid becoming conscripts they emigrate. In many instances they have sold their household effects In order to raise their passage money. Of the vast number of Italian immigrants now on Ellis Island but a very small proportion are ticketed through to railroad towns.
