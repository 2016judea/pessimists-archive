TASK: research historical cases where warnings/fears/skepticism about a new technology, product,
substance, financial innovation or idea proved RIGHT (verdict fear_right) or PARTLY right (verdict mixed).
Counterweight to the Pessimists Archive (which only catalogues fears that proved wrong). Used for business
ideation to avoid survivorship bias. Be honest: if the evidence says the fear was mostly wrong, say so and
DROP the case (report it), or mark mixed if genuinely partial.

OUTPUT: one JSON object per line (JSONL), appended to YOUR part file as you finish each case
(write incrementally, do not batch at the end). Use python3 json.dumps(obj, ensure_ascii=False) to append,
so escaping is valid. Fields, exactly these keys:

case_id           slug, e.g. "thalidomide-1961"
thing             what was new
domain            one of: media|computing|transport|energy|medicine|food|finance|work|consumer|science|culture
year              int, when the warning was voiced (first strong statement)
claim             one plain line: the warning
claim_type        one of: health|youth_morals|jobs|safety|wont_work|isolation|decline|environment|financial|privacy|security|legal
warner            one of: press|physicians|scientists|clergy|incumbents|unions|government|intellectuals|public|investors|insiders
incumbent_stake   bool: did the warner stand to lose if the thing won
verdict           fear_right | mixed
what_happened     one line
years_to_verdict  int
dismissed_by      who waved the warning away (one short line)
cost_of_error     one line, with numbers where known (deaths, $ losses, settlements)
modern_analog     array of strings, from e.g.: AI, social media, crypto, GLP-1, EVs, self-driving, gene editing,
                  nuclear, vaping, ultra-processed food, sports betting, smartphones, LLM chatbots, private credit,
                  stablecoins, drones, AR/VR, microplastics, space tourism, longevity, fintech, gig economy
lesson            one transferable sentence for someone deciding today
quotes            array of {"text": verbatim, "speaker", "year": int, "publication", "url"}
sources           array of urls (prefer regulators, courts, peer-reviewed retrospectives, major newspapers)
confidence        high | medium | low

HARD RULES (why: this feeds a dataset whose whole value is that it can be audited):
1. Verify each case with at least one source you actually FETCHED (WebFetch or firecrawl_scrape),
   not just a search snippet. Put fetched urls in sources.
2. Quotes must be VERBATIM text that appears on a page you fetched, and the url field must be that page.
   Prefer the WARNER's own words (e.g. Boisjoly's 1985 memo, Markopolos's SEC submission, Rachel Carson,
   a regulator's report quoting the warning). Copy-paste exactly; do not tidy, translate or paraphrase.
   If you cannot find a verbatim warner quote on a fetched page, set quotes to [] and confidence "medium".
   confidence "high" = verbatim warner quote fetched + authoritative source; "low" = thin evidence.
3. Honest verdicts: "mixed" when the fear was real but overstated, or right about harm but wrong about scale/
   adoption, or the evidence is still contested. Do not upgrade a contested case to fear_right.
4. Keep every text field to one line. No newlines inside values.
5. Budget: ~2-4 tool calls per case. Wikipedia is acceptable as a fetched page for a quote only if the quote
   is attributed there; prefer primary/regulator/newspaper pages. Good sources for quotes: Wikipedia articles
   that quote memos, history.nasa.gov, sec.gov, congress.gov, fda.gov, nytimes/guardian/ft archives,
   PubMed/PMC retrospectives.

TOOLS: load WebFetch/WebSearch with ToolSearch ("select:WebFetch,WebSearch") or use
mcp__firecrawl__firecrawl_search and mcp__firecrawl__firecrawl_scrape (formats ["markdown"]). If one tool
fails on a site, try the other.

FINAL REPLY (short, under 8 lines): number of cases written, case_ids written, cases dropped and why.
